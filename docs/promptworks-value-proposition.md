# Promptworks — value proposition

**Audience:** CFOs, COOs, and the executive who was told to show a return on the AI spend.
**Purpose:** the argument, on one page. Not a feature list.

---

## The problem you already have

- You bought AI licences for most of the company. That line item is visible.
- Almost nobody's actual work changed. That part is not visible, which is worse.
- You cannot currently answer three questions your board will ask: *Who is actually using it? Are they any good at it? What did we get back?*
- The default fix is a training vendor — videos, a completion certificate, a 92% "completion rate" that measures attendance, not skill.

## What Promptworks is

- A practice platform. Employees work real scenarios from their own job function, write the prompt, and get scored against a fixed six-point rubric.
- A workflow library. The prompts that score well are saved as fill-in-the-blank templates the whole team runs — including the people who never practiced.

## Value proposition, by who is asking

**For the employee**
- Stop guessing. Get a score, a corrected version, and the reason it was better.
- Ten minutes a week, not a half-day workshop.

**For the manager**
- See who is genuinely proficient, dimension by dimension — not who finished the video.
- Find the team's weakest skill and assign the fix in one click.

**For the business**
- A measurable skill baseline that moves, plus a library of validated prompts your team actually uses.
- Both are assets. One of them stays when the employee leaves.

## What this gives you that a training vendor does not

- **A number that means something.** Median score out of 30, per person, per skill dimension, tracked over time. Completion rates measure attendance; this measures capability.
- **An asset, not an expense.** Training buys you a claim about human capital that walks out the door with the human. A workflow library is inventory — it stays, it compounds, and new hires inherit it on day one.
- **Reach beyond the volunteers.** Realistically ~15% of any team will practice. The workflow library is how the other 85% produce expert-quality output anyway — one person gets the prompt right, forty people run it.
- **A cost line that goes down.** Prompt efficiency is scored. A team whose median prompt burns 38% more tokens than necessary for the same output quality is a fixable, quantified expense.

## How the return is calculated

- **Hard numbers, reported as facts:** seats active, reps completed, median rubric score and its month-over-month change, workflow runs, unique users per workflow, helpful-rating per workflow, token cost per run.
- **Estimated numbers, labelled as estimates:** hours returned = (workflow runs) × (minutes saved per run) × (loaded hourly rate). Every input is set by you and visible on the dashboard.
- We do not hand you a pre-cooked ROI figure. You set the assumptions, you can change them, and you can defend the result to your own board because you built it.

## Why this does not churn like the last training subscription

- Training gets completed, and completed things get cancelled. That is the entire history of the category.
- Workflows get used. A service team running a validated denial-explanation prompt nine times a shift does not cancel it.
- The practice half creates the quality signal. The workflow half creates the dependency. Neither works alone, which is also why it is hard to copy.

## Objections worth raising before you do

- *"Prompting will matter less as models improve."* Possibly true. The workflow library is the hedge — a versioned library of validated, measured AI workflows keeps its value even if the term goes out of fashion.
- *"Our people won't use it."* Most won't practice. That is priced in and it is why the workflow library exists. Adoption is reported honestly, including the people who never log in.
- *"Our data."* Prompts and outputs are never used to train models. SSO/SAML, SCIM provisioning, configurable retention. Security review before signature, not after.
- *"We already have a shared prompt doc."* Everyone does, and nobody opens it, because nothing in it has a quality score, a version, an owner, or a usage count. That is the difference.

## The proposal

- **Pilot:** one team, 25–40 seats, 90 days.
- **Scope:** 15 scenarios written from that team's real work, the six-dimension rubric, the team dashboard, and the workflow library.
- **Baseline on day one:** every participant's starting rubric score, recorded before any training. Without it there is nothing to compare to and no honest claim to make at the end.
- **What we report at day 90:** change in median score, per-dimension movement, workflows published and their run counts, token cost per run, and hours returned on your assumptions.
- **Kill criterion, agreed up front:** if median score has not moved and fewer than five workflows are in active use, the pilot failed and you do not renew. Stated in writing.
